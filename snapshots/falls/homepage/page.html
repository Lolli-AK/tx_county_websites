Essential Safety Precautions After a Burn Ban Is Lifted
  • Never leave a fire unattended: A responsible adult capable of putting out the fire must stay with it at all times until it is completely out.
  • Keep extinguishing tools nearby: Always have a water source (like a charged garden hose), a bucket of water, a shovel, or a fire extinguisher right next to the burn site.
  • Check the weather (avoid windy or dry days): Never burn when it is windy, as gusty conditions can easily carry sparks and ignite dry grass or brush.
  • Mind clearance distances: Keep burn piles small and maintain safe distances away from structures, fences, trees, and wooded areas (recommended at least 25 to 150+ feet).

  • Burn only approved materials: Strictly burn permitted natural debris (such as clean brush or tree limbs). Never burn household trash, plastics, heavy oils, or construction materials, and do not haul in debris from elsewhere.
  • Watch motorized equipment: Sparks from lawnmowers, chain saws, and off-road vehicles driving on dry grass can still trigger fast-moving grass fires.
  • Burn Responsibly!

Falls County, Texas, is a Central Texas community with a strong agricultural foundation and a rich historical heritage. Centered around the county seat of Marlin, the county is home to rural landscapes, the Brazos River, and a network of small communities that contribute to its local character. Falls County is committed to providing essential services that support public safety, infrastructure, and the well-being of its residents, while preserving the traditions that define the area.
