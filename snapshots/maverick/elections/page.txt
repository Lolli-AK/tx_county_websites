Voter Registration
Check, register and get ready to vote
Voter registration in Texas is county-based. Here you'll find how to confirm whether you're already registered, how to register for the first time, what you need, and which ID to bring when you vote.
Registration deadline
Monday, October 5, 2026 for the November 3 General Election
The postmark date or the date the office receives your application counts. Tip: ask for a free manual (local) postmark at a USPS retail counter so the date matches the day you mail it.
Step 1
Am I Registered?
Look up your record in the Texas Secretary of State's official portal. You can search by your Voter Unique Identifier (VUID, printed on your voter registration certificate), your Texas driver's license number, or your first and last name.
The portal also shows your polling places a few days before each election.
Check my registration
Step 2
Register to Vote
Fill out the official application online, print it, sign it, and mail it or deliver it in person to the Maverick County Elections Department.
The online form is not submitted electronically — it must be printed and sent to the voter registrar.
Start application
Moved?
Update Your Info
If you changed your name or moved within the county, you can update your registration online through Texas.gov or in writing with the voter registrar.
If you moved to another county, you must re-register in your new county.
Update online
Eligibility
Who can register?
Be a United States citizen.
Be a resident of the county where you submit the application (Maverick County).
Be at least 17 years and 10 months old when you apply, and 18 years old on or before Election Day.
Not be finally convicted of a felony — or, if you are a felon, have completed all of your punishment, including any term of incarceration, parole, supervision, period of probation, or have received a pardon.
Not have been determined by a final judgment of a court exercising probate jurisdiction to be totally mentally incapacitated or partially mentally incapacitated without the right to vote.
Have ready
What do you need for the application?
Full name
First, middle and last name (include suffix Jr., Sr., III), plus former name if any.
Date of birth
Formatted mm/dd/yyyy.
Residence address
Where you live. No P.O. Box, rural route or business address.
Mailing & previous address
Only if your mailing address is different or you have moved.
Identification number
One of these: Texas driver's license or Texas ID card number (DPS); if not issued, the last 4 digits of your Social Security Number; or check the box stating you have neither.
Signature & date
You sign under penalty of perjury. Giving false information is a crime under state and federal law.
After you apply
What happens next?
1
Submit your application
By mail (USPS) or in person. It must be postmarked or received by the 30th day before the election.
2
Registrar review
If information is missing, you will get a notice in the mail with a deadline to respond.
3
Registration takes effect
30 days after the application is submitted, or on your 18th birthday, whichever is later.
4
Receive your certificate
It arrives by mail. Read it, sign by the X in the yellow area, and keep it in a safe place.
To vote in person
Acceptable photo IDs
1
Texas Driver License (DPS)
2
Texas Election Identification Certificate (EIC, DPS)
3
Texas Personal Identification Card (DPS)
4
Texas Handgun License (DPS)
5
U.S. Military ID card with photograph
6
U.S. Citizenship Certificate with photograph
7
U.S. Passport (book or card)
For voters aged 18–69, the ID may be expired no more than 4 years. Voters 70 or older may use an ID expired for any length of time if otherwise valid. The Citizenship Certificate does not expire.
Don't have one and can't reasonably get one?
You can still vote a regular ballot by signing a Reasonable Impediment Declaration and presenting a supporting document, such as a copy or original of a government document showing your name and address (including your voter registration certificate), a current utility bill, bank statement, government check, paycheck, or a certified birth certificate.
If you have an acceptable ID but forgot it, you must return with it — the declaration does not apply.
More about voter ID
Election Identification Certificate (DPS)
Registration FAQs
Where can I get a paper application?
At the Maverick County Elections Department (501 E. Main St., Suite B, Eagle Pass), libraries, most post offices, high schools, DPS offices and Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC) offices. You can also request a postage-paid application from the State.
My status says “Suspense.” What does that mean?
It means the registrar is not certain of your current residence address — for example, because your certificate or other mail was returned as undeliverable. You can still vote by completing a Statement of Residence when you vote within the same county.
I moved to a new county and missed the deadline. Can I still vote?
You may be eligible to vote a limited ballot in your new county, only during early voting at the main early voting polling place, as long as you are still registered in your former county. It is not available on Election Day.
Do I need to bring my voter registration certificate to vote?
It is not required, but it is highly recommended. In some situations it allows you to vote a regular ballot instead of a provisional ballot. If you lost it, request a replacement from the voter registrar.
Who can vote by mail?
Only registered voters who: will be away from the county on Election Day and during early voting hours; are sick or disabled; are expected to give birth within three weeks before or after Election Day; are 65 or older on Election Day; or are confined in jail or under involuntary civil commitment.
I'm 17. Can I register?
Yes, once you are 17 years and 10 months old, as long as you will be 18 on or before Election Day. Your registration takes effect on your 18th birthday if that falls after the 30-day period.
