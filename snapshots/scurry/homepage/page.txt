Welcome to Scurry County
Scurry County, nestled in west central Texas, covers about 905.4 square miles below the Caprock in the Panhandle and West Texas region. Snyder, the county seat, sits roughly 80 miles southeast of Lubbock and 85 miles northwest of Abilene. It's named after Confederate General William Scurry and was established in 1876, getting fully organized in 1884. As of 2020, it's home to around 16,932 folks.

About Scurry County
Scurry County is located in west-central Texas, known for its rich history and contributions to the state's development. Established in 1876 and organized in 1884, the county was named after William R. Scurry, a Confederate general in the Civil War. Its seat, Snyder, became a focal point for settlement due to its proximity to Clear Fork of the Brazos River and a convenient location for ranching and trade. Early inhabitants included Native American tribes such as the Comanches, who thrived in the region until the late 19th century when settlers and military campaigns pushed them out.
The county's history is deeply tied to its cattle ranching heritage. In the late 19th century, vast open ranges made Scurry County ideal for cattle grazing, attracting settlers seeking new opportunities in the rugged Texas landscape. The development of the railroad further spurred growth, allowing for the transportation of cattle and goods to markets across the state and beyond. By the early 20th century, oil discoveries transformed the county’s economy, bringing an influx of workers and industry. Snyder became a boomtown during the 1950s due to the discovery of the Canyon Reef oil field, one of the largest at the time.
Today, Scurry County reflects a blend of its historical roots and modern developments. While agriculture and energy production remain vital to its economy, the county also celebrates its history through museums, local festivals, and preserved historical landmarks. The rich cultural heritage and resilience of the people make Scurry County a unique and integral part of Texas history.
